# Original Marbleworks No.145 snake ramp

Sources:

Reconstruction: four tangent alternating approximately 263° arcs, with the complete U-channel sheared onto one world-space slope plane, rounded undersides, open lobe centers, blended end-column junctions, closed inlet cup and through-bore outlet. Y is up. Outer molds are united before subtracting all channel cores; neighboring outside walls cannot intrude into the running channel.

All fit dimensions come from marbleworks-spec.ts: 27 mm body, 24 mm male, 24.2 mm socket, 47 mm stacking rise, 15 mm insertion, 140.071 mm port-center spacing, 20 mm channel and 13 mm floor drop. Bend centers advance by one quarter of the shared span. Their 23.368 mm centerline radius leaves 0.3 mm of geometric separation before blending the outer lips of same-side curls. Each contact is a 3D smooth union of the actual U-section signed-distance fields, including the rolled lip and rounded underside. It has no separately extruded neck, rectangular clipping faces exposed at the join, or flat bridge floor. Channel cores are subtracted afterwards. The lateral center offset and sweep angle follow from tangency between successive centerline arcs; exact 270° arcs were an incorrect constraint that made the outside walls overlap. Every track vertex uses y = inletFloor − 13 × (x/span + 0.5) plus its section height, so both rims share the same slope in side view. This replaces the previous travel-distance height rule. These are fitted reconstruction dimensions, not factory measurements; the design patent has no dimensional scale. Each end connection is a broad neck with two concave 7 mm rolling-circle fillets tangent to the column and neighboring band. The top photograph shows both sides flaring into those round bodies. A local rounded level-set solid supplies the web; the original channel cores are subtracted afterwards. The 7 mm fillet is a visual fit, not a measured factory dimension. Mold lettering is not reconstructed.

Review: /snake.html. Clearance verification uses a nominal 15.9 mm marble at 161 channel positions plus eight positions through the outlet, and requires a single valid solid.

The inlet landing floor uses the same slope as the track. Its shear fades out above the bottom fillet to retain the shared connector bore. Coplanar floor vertices use the analytical plane normal to avoid radial-fan shading at the lathed cup. Fifteen ray probes verify the landing disk is coplanar.

The inlet floor is derived from the 47 mm connector seat, channel depth, rolled lip and the uphill lobe overhang, with 0.05 mm clearance. It is not the straight ramp's 37 mm datum. Tests check both actual stacked spacer solids for interference and reject track vertices above the seat outside the male connector.

End necks blend into the receiving band and cylinder in elevation as well as plan. Redundant radial opening cutters were removed because they disagreed with the swept channel core. Construction face boundaries are removed before two 0.001 mm cleanup passes. Render checks reject collapsed Float32 triangles and excessive sliver triangles; they do not certify visual smoothness. Smooth channel trim vertices use shared area-weighted normals.

The preview loads `src/generated/snake.bin` instead of executing CAD operations in the browser. After changing `snake-solid.ts` or shared dimensions, run `bun run geometry:snake` to rebuild the saved positions, normals and indices. Commit the binary with its source changes. Ordinary preview reloads and material/camera changes need no geometry rebuild.

The geometry build runs middle and end blend sampling in two Bun workers, transfers the resulting mesh buffers, then performs the final CSG and cleanup in the main process. The two identical middle contacts share one sampled template. Resolution and cleanup tolerances are unchanged. The render audit reuses the final normals. Measured full builds on this machine decreased from 4.74 s to 3.19–3.22 s; these timings exclude the optional full clearance suite. Run `bun run geometry:snake --verify` for the worker-built result plus clearance tests; `--serial` is available for diagnosis. Every build reports its stage timings.

End-neck revision: the sampled end patches are replaced by explicit lofts of two tangent circle arcs. Each height row uses the U-section's actual outer radius; straight closing chords sit inside the receiving solids. This avoids a second curved receiver skin and the thin flange/air-gap seam it could create. A ray regression checks that the affected wall has exactly two crossings. The local middle blends remain sampled. Cleanup uses 0.01 mm, followed by conservative diagonal flips with a 0.005 mm coplanarity bound. No vertex positions move during the flips. The full worker-built result passes the existing clearance and rim-plane suite. Current measured full build: about 4.6 s and 139k triangles.

The loft's rolled upper edge now uses parallel offsets of its wall outline: the receiving radii shrink by the lip inset while the concave fillet radius grows by the same amount. This preserves the track's 0.75 mm circular section throughout the join. A geometric regression measures that radius along both bridges. Tube-side normals converge continuously onto the analytical cylinder normal over 0.75 mm, replacing the abrupt position-based normal switch; seat and cut-plane normals remain separate.
